Ph.D. Dissertation

Representation Learning for Biological Inference from Single-Cell Transcriptomics

Sanghun Sel

Department of Information Convergence Engineering
The Graduate School, Pusan National University
Advisor: Giltae Song
February 2027

Abstract

This dissertation develops representation-learning approaches that improve cell annotation, prioritize targets for individual transcription factors, and reconstruct stimulation-associated expression from compact gene panels. The studies address three practical challenges in single-cell analysis: limited annotation, incomplete regulatory evidence, and restricted molecular measurement. Each representation is designed around the information needed for its prediction task, and each contribution is evaluated through matched comparisons, component controls, and biologically relevant summaries. Together, the studies connect representation design to measurable improvements in single-cell transcriptomic analysis.

The first investigation improves annotation by combining measured expression with a low-rank representation learned within each training split. SVD initialization followed by pseudo-Huber matrix factorization constructs the auxiliary view while retaining the same gene coordinates. A shared transformer processes aligned subvectors, incorporates a factor-derived relation term, and averages the two sets of logits for classification. Evaluation used 13 public datasets and five fixed seeds. With a total label budget targeting 10% and realized dataset means of 10.01–11.39%, aggregate Macro-F1 was 0.8820, compared with 0.8637 and 0.8665 for independently fitted measured-only and reconstructed-only models. The combined model surpassed both alternatives in 11 datasets. Component controls left the separate advantages of robust refinement and relation mixing unresolved, while independent-view comparisons support the annotation benefit of combining the two representations under the evaluated label budget.

The second investigation combines gene representations, training-derived graph context, and ordered decoding to rank regulatory interactions across a network and targets within individual TFs. PBMC and lymphoma inputs combine RNA and aligned gene activity. Across six datasets, pooled average precision was competitive with the learned comparators, while mean TF-specific retrieval was highest among seven methods. Favorable ranking was also observed in a separate complete-candidate analysis. Comparisons remained descriptive after multiplicity correction. Graph context mainly supported pooled discrimination, while ordered scoring was associated with early retrieval. Degree and input controls identify a substantial prior contribution and limit attribution to measured expression. Seed recurrence and PBMC examples complement these prior-informed, transductive rankings and support candidate selection for experimental follow-up.

The third investigation improves response reconstruction by selecting 100 measured genes for 200 disjoint targets. Panel-only normalization, donor-level response error, and source cell-type constraints guide a bounded gene-swap search. A common target-query transformer reconstructs expression in the observed control or stimulated state. Following three development splits, external response MSE across five donors was 0.2979, representing reductions of 4.78% from mixed initialization, 4.73% from matched cell-error selection, and 19.62% from a supervision-expanded selector adaptation. The selected panels had the lowest mean response, residual, and cell MSE among eight benchmark configurations. Six substitutions per source panel produced these gains, and CD4 and CD8 T-cell response errors improved in all five donors relative to initialization. Cell identity and response amplitude remain distinct evaluation outcomes, and additional comparisons using the same external cohort are exploratory. Together, the studies improve annotation, identify favorable TF-specific retrieval patterns, and recover more response information from limited measurements.

Keywords: single-cell transcriptomics; representation learning; cell identity; regulatory prioritization; gene panel selection; response reconstruction
