Dissertation Overview

This dissertation investigates representation learning strategies for extracting biologically meaningful information from single-cell transcriptomic data. The work focuses on how learned representations can support three complementary forms of biological inference: robust cellular characterization, transcription-factor-centered regulatory prioritization, and compact gene measurement that preserves biologically relevant variation.

Across the dissertation, the central question is how transcriptomic representations can be designed to retain the biological signals that matter for downstream inference while reducing noise, dimensionality, and task-specific limitations. The studies collectively connect cellular identity, gene regulation, and targeted measurement within a unified representation-learning perspective.

01

Research Motivation

Single-cell RNA sequencing provides high-dimensional measurements of cellular state, but biological interpretation remains challenging because useful signals are distributed across noisy, sparse, and highly redundant gene-expression profiles.

Cellular Representation

Reliable representations should preserve information that distinguishes biologically meaningful cell populations even under limited supervision and incomplete observations.

Regulatory Inference

Regulatory analysis requires prioritizing candidate target genes in a transcription-factor-aware manner rather than relying only on aggregate edge-level performance.

Compact Measurement

Practical assays often measure only a small subset of genes, creating a need for compact gene panels that retain both cell identity and stimulus-response information.

02

Research Components

The dissertation consists of three connected studies addressing representation learning at complementary levels of single-cell transcriptomic analysis.

Study I

Cell Identity Representation from Single-Cell Transcriptomics

This study investigates transcriptomic representation learning for cell-level inference by combining measured expression with reconstructed transcriptomic information. The framework is designed to improve the robustness of cellular representations under constrained label availability while avoiding information leakage in downstream evaluation.

Published in BioData Mining.

Paper link Code repository
Study II

Transcription Factor–Aware Gene Regulatory Inference

This study develops a transcription-factor-aware framework for regulatory candidate-edge prioritization. It combines graph-derived information and transcriptomic features to rank putative target genes for individual transcription factors, with emphasis on TF-specific prioritization rather than only pooled interaction-level evaluation.

Code repository
Study III

Compact Gene Panel Selection for Cell Identity and Perturbation Response

This study investigates selection of a compact gene panel for joint preservation of cell identity and perturbation response. The study examines whether a small common set of measured genes can retain information needed for both cellular classification and response-sensitive biological inference, while supporting reconstruction of unmeasured expression.

Ongoing manuscript preparation.

03

Results and Performance

The three studies evaluate representation quality from complementary perspectives: cellular discrimination, TF-centered ranking, and information retention under compact gene measurement.

Cell Identity Representation from Single-Cell Transcriptomics

The study evaluates cell-level prediction under strict label-budget settings and compares measured-only, reconstructed-only, and dual-view representations using consistent folds and random seeds. Macro-F1 is used as the primary evaluation metric.

Transcription Factor–Aware Gene Regulatory Inference

TRACE is evaluated using both pooled and per-TF metrics, including AUPRC and TF-specific precision-oriented ranking measures. The analysis emphasizes the distinction between overall candidate-edge discrimination and transcription-factor-specific target prioritization.

Compact Gene Panel Selection for Cell Identity and Perturbation Response

ReSPAR evaluates the trade-off between panel size and biological information preservation, focusing on whether compact gene sets can jointly maintain cell-type structure and perturbation-response signals.

Dissertation-Level Contribution

Together, the studies demonstrate how task-aware transcriptomic representations can be used to support biological inference across cell identity, gene regulation, and targeted gene measurement.
